## Brief used

- **Who:** Dublin and Irish drivers of BMW, Audi, Mercedes, VW and Japanese imports. Mostly on phones, often arriving from Google.
- **Job to be done:** "Get my phone on the car's own screen without making a mess of the car."
- **Current discomfort (their words, from how people describe it):** a phone in a mount on the vent, a cable across the console, a factory screen that does nothing useful.
- **Objections:** Will it look aftermarket? Will I lose the factory screen or buttons? What will it cost? How long will the car be off the road? Can I trust the fitter?
- **MG's edges (all real):** fixed fitted prices (BMW CarPlay €350 fitted, Android Auto €299, Android radio fitting €150), most fits 1 to 2 hours, Dublin 12 workshop, 4.9 from 57 Google reviews, honest advice.
- **Customer language from the Google reviews in `templates/index.json`:** "very tidy work", "completed within the hour", "very honest guys", "fixed my screen in no time", "works perfectly", "well priced".
- **What sits right under the hero (trust bar):** 4.9 from 57 Google reviews · Fitted in Dublin 12, most jobs in 1 to 2 hours · Fixed fitted prices, agreed before we start · Workmanship warranty. So the hero doesn't need to repeat location, time or price proof: it can spend its words on the promise and the objection.

### Why option 1 wins

- **Clarity over cleverness:** four plain words per idea. "Keep" and "Add" are the two things a visitor needs to know: nothing is taken away, CarPlay is added.
- **Loss aversion and status-quo bias (marketing-psychology):** people value what they already have (a factory dash, a factory screen) more than what they might gain. Leading with "Keep" removes the biggest fear of retrofits (a cut-up dash, a cheap-looking aftermarket radio) before asking for anything.
- **Contrast effect (subline):** "No phone mount, no dangling cable" paints the before state the visitor is living with right now, so the after state (their phone on the big screen) is vivid without adjectives. That is the Human Action Model spine: discomfort (subline), vision (headline), path (buttons).
- **"Now you can…" test:** "Now you can keep your dash and add CarPlay" is compelling and true for the core service (CarPlay and Android Auto on the factory screen).
- **Fits the design rules:** 2 lines on desktop and on a 390px phone at a larger size than before (the old headline took 3 lines on both); subline 17 words; no em dashes, no hype words, no exclamation marks.
- **Proof sits one scroll-free step away:** the trust bar attached under the hero carries the 4.9 rating, Dublin 12, 1 to 2 hours and fixed prices, and the sticker carries the one real headline price (BMW CarPlay €350 fitted). The hero doesn't repeat them.

### Runner-ups

1. **Phone mount out. / CarPlay in.** (25) Punchy and visual, but "out/in" reads a little clever on first glance. Worth an A/B test against the winner.
2. **CarPlay fitted / within the hour.** (25) The customers' own words and a strong speed claim, but only some jobs take under an hour (most take 1 to 2 hours), so it fails the "true" check as a blanket promise. Use it in a review quote, not as the headline.
3. **BMW CarPlay. / €350 fitted.** (27, too narrow for home) Use on the BMW page and BMW ads.

## CTAs

- **Main: "See fitted prices"** (3 words, action verb + what they get). Fixed, published fitted prices are MG's clearest edge (Irish competitors mostly say "call for a quote"), and it is a low-commitment first step (foot-in-the-door) for visitors who are still comparing. It goes to the CarPlay & Android Auto collection, where each product shows its fitted price.
- **Second: "Book a fitting"** (3 words). Same label as the header, phone tab bar and every other booking link (one label per intent).
- **WhatsApp:** moved out of the hero text stack. It is now an always-visible green "WhatsApp us" pill in the bottom-right corner on tablets and desktops (`snippets/mg-whatsapp-button.liquid`, no hover needed) and a green-tinted WhatsApp tab in the phone tab bar (`snippets/mg-mobile-action-bar.liquid`), so it is in the first screen on every device without a third hero action. The hero keeps its "Show WhatsApp link" setting (off by default) for anyone who wants the "Or WhatsApp us a photo of your dash" line back.
- The hero now has exactly one primary and one secondary action (design-taste-frontend hero stack rule).
